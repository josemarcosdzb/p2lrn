3. Before redistributing

If you distribute first-party components covered by the license, check the following under sections 8 and 9 and Annex A.2:

  1. Retain LICENSE, copyright and attribution notices, NOTICE.md, and applicable third-party licenses.
  2. Identify modifications and their date; do not represent a derivative as an official release.
  3. Provide corresponding source code when distributing modified versions or covered software in non-source form, such as binaries or executables.
  4. Keep source accessible, when offered by URL for a public distribution, throughout distribution and for at least three years after that version's last public distribution.
  5. Separate third-party rights: Annex A does not relicense H5P, images, libraries, other authors' courses or dependencies.

4. Common situations

Practical explanations to help you choose how to use each component.

Can I create and sell .p2lrn courses?

Yes. Annex A.1 permits creating, validating, distributing and selling .p2lrn documents; A.4 recognizes authors' rights in their original content. You must respect any third-party rights in materials you include.

Can a private training academy use Studio and Analytics for paid courses?

Yes, with unmodified official versions for the institution's own activities. Annex A.3 permits configuring public options and connecting documented integrations.

Can I integrate Player or Toolkit into a commercial platform?

Yes, for first-party code covered by Annex A.2. If you distribute covered software, you must retain the license and notices, identify changes and meet corresponding source code obligations.

Can I modify Studio or Analytics and offer them as SaaS?

Not under the institutional permission in Annex A.3. Modifying, creating derivatives or incorporating them into SaaS, an LMS or a commercial service offered to third parties requires prior express authorization from the rights holder.

When must I provide corresponding source code?

When redistributing covered software in non-source form, or distributing modified versions, under section 9. If corresponding source is offered by URL for a public distribution, it must remain accessible during distribution and for at least three years after the final public distribution of that version.

Is this an OSI-approved open-source license?

No. P2LRN-NCSL 1.0 is source-available and retains restrictions; Annex A grants specific exceptions. Third-party components keep their own licenses.

9. Your courses remain yours

Creating, converting or playing content with P2LRN does not transfer ownership to the project. You can decide how to distribute or sell your original materials while respecting applicable third-party rights and any included software licenses.

10. Uses that require permission

To modify Studio or Analytics, offer them as SaaS, or include them in commercial products or services for third parties, you need prior express permission. Use the contact form on this website to request it. Enter “P2LRN commercial license request” as the subject and explain the components, versions, intended changes and whether you will offer them to third parties. Submitting the form does not grant a license; an express written agreement is required.
