Create interactive courses online easily and quickly

P2LRN Studio brings together lessons, activities and learner view in one visual editor. You can prepare a course, test it, and export it as a .p2lrn package to play with Player.

FROM EDITOR TO COURSE

How it works, step by step

  1. 01

    Create the course and its structure

    In Studio, press “Create Course” and organize the units and lessons that the learner will see.

  2. 02

    Add content and activities

    Edit text and images from the interface and use “Add Content” to add tests and compatible games.

  3. 03

    Check the learner view

    Toggle between editing and learner View to review the output before distributing it.

  4. 04

    Export the package

    Download the .p2lrn file and open it with Player or integrate it into the target environment.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Questions about creating and sharing courses

Do I need to install a platform or program to create courses?

No. You can create lessons, quizzes and activities with P2LRN Studio and export them as a portable package. You only need a compatible environment to play the course.

Can I create and share quizzes without an educational platform?

Yes. You can include assessments in your course and play them with Player. Centralized enrolment management and official grading depend on the services connected to your course.

Can I distribute a course without an internet connection?

Yes, using a supported offline delivery mode. Learners can open the course locally and return results later in a .p2lrn-result file, depending on the delivery mode.

Can I combine lessons, quizzes and games in one course?

Yes. Studio lets you organize different kinds of content and activities in a single package, creating a consistent learning journey.

Can I reuse the course on a website or learning platform?

The .p2lrn format is designed for portability. Use it with Player or integrate it into compatible environments; features such as identity and official grades require additional integration.
